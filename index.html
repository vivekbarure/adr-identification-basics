ADR Identification Basics

Learn to identify possible Adverse Drug Reactions.

Learning note: These are fictional training scenarios, not medical advice. Serious suspected reactions need urgent professional assessment.

How to identify an ADR

  1. Review the patient's symptoms and medical history.
  2. Check when the medicine was started or changed.
  3. Compare the medicine timeline with symptom onset.
  4. Consider other possible causes.
  5. Document the suspected medicine and seek clinical review.

Case 1: Itching and rash

A 45-year-old patient starts a new antibiotic. Two hours after the first dose, an itchy rash appears. No other new medicines are reported.

Case 2: Dizziness after a dose change

A patient's blood-pressure medicine dose is increased. The next day, the patient reports dizziness when standing up.

Case 3: Nausea before treatment

A patient reports nausea that began two days before starting a new medicine. The nausea continues afterward.

Key Takeaways

  • Symptoms after starting medicine may suggest an ADR.
  • Timing alone cannot prove that a medicine caused a symptom.
  • Check the medicine name, dose, dates and other medicines.
  • Document and report suspected reactions appropriately.
  • Seek professional guidance before changing treatment.
